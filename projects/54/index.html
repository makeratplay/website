It Starts With an Idea

The 2022 Marching Show was revealed in April. This year's show is Zentangle.

The show reveal marks the start of the band props season for me and all the band parent volunteers.

I was told props were going to be easy this year. "We will use the frames from last year's props and hang vinyl on them". There is no fun in that so I had to add more of a challenge. Well, maybe that isn't exactly what happened.

A key visual part of this year's show is the graphics on the field going from black and white to full color. Kinda like a coloring book that starts with just an outline and then is colored in. The two mockup images below convey the idea but are not the real artwork for the show. There will be eight 10-foot circles upright on the field with an additional large vinyl circle laying on the ground under a stage. The eight upright props will be rotated during the show to change from black and white to color.

Let's Fire Up Fusion 360 and Design Something

I know that I need a 10 foot round frame to hold a vinyl print and the frame will be supported by last year's prop frames. That is easy enough to create in Fusion 360.

I sent the above mockup to the team coordinating the show to start the conversation on prop design. And received back the following comment

As you work through this, the circle props will need to “flip.” One side will have one design, the other side will have another. We can defer to you in terms of the logistics of that as long as we know how many students it will need to make it happen.

Which lead to my response of

Your use of the word "flip" made me rethink one of my assumptions. In previous years we have "rotated" the props to switch to the backside, but maybe flipping the props is what we want this year.

With this new thought I mocked up two animated examples for flipping vs rotating to see which is preferred by the team.

Flip is the way to go! Less effort for the students to flip the prop than to rotate which means only one student is needed per prop and as a added bonus, it just looks cooler.

We also need to think about how these props will be transported from contest to contest. We are on a budget so we can't require a lot of trucks to haul everything. We need to design these to fit into a single 26 foot box truck.

Designed to Travel

We will reuse the wheel bases from last year's props . This becomes our starting point for this year's design. Improving on what we learned last year, this year's Props will use hitch pins instead of nuts and bolts allowing them to be assebled and disassembled without tools. The frames will also be easy to remove from the bases allowing them to be packed closer together in the truck.

Will It Work? Prototyping Time

We need eight props but it starts with building just one (AKA The Prototype) to prove that our design will actually work. Until the prototype is built, the design is just an idea that might need to change. It's 4th of July weekend and 100+ degrees outside, let's do some welding 😁

And here is the functional prototype. It worked and worked well!

That's A Lot of Metal Pieces

Once we have a design and built our prototype to ensure it will work, it's time for some mass production to build the remaining seven Props. It starts with a trip to Metal Supermarket in Lewisville to order all the metal.

Let The Real Work Begin

First step in the assembly line entails cutting all the material to length and drilling all the required holes. Normally the metal would come already cut to length from the Metal Supermarket except in this case you might have noticed there are a lot of angles in the project. The Metal Supermarket cuts only one angle, 90 degrees. We needed angles like 22.4 degrees. Time for our first Props work session to make fast work of getting all the metal to final size for welding.

Weld And Weld Some More

368 individual pieces have to be welded together to make all eight props. There are 456 joints that must be welded. Using jigs and a well defined workflow makes fast repeatable work of all this.

It's Really All About the Vinyl

The frames are cool and were fun to design and build, but the real star of the show for these props is the vinyl that will hang on the frames. We don't have the vinyl yet but we do have the vinyl from the 2018 They Walk Among Us show. We can use it to prototype how we will attach the vinyl to our frames.

We are thinking that we will use self tapping screws to attach the bottom half of the vinyl permanently to the PVC frame and put grommets in the top half of the vinyl for zip/velcro ties to attach to the upper frame. We will put screws/grommets every 12 inches around the vinyl to align with the frame.

We Did Say They Have to Travel

Hallelujah! After two solid weeks of welding all the metal work is done. It is time to clear out my garage and move these props to their proper storage location at the high school. It only took two trips to get all eight props moved.

Time To Paint

Now that all the metal work is done, it's time to put a coat of paint on everything.

We Have Full Circles

This Labor day weekend, we held another workday to fully assemble the props minus the vinyl.

Vinyl Has Arrived!

A couple hundred pounds of vinyl arrived via pallet. We commandeer the band hall for a couple of nights to cut and attach all the vinyl
